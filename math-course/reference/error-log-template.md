# Error Log

The highest-leverage habit in the program. Every missed problem gets one line.

Copy this into a notebook or spreadsheet. Handwriting is fine and probably better.

---

## The categories

| Code | Category | Meaning | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| `C` | Concept | Didn't know or understand the method | Slow down; re-study with worked examples |
| `A` | Algebra | Knew the method; the algebra broke | **Stop and drill that specific skill** |
| `R` | Arithmetic | Sign error, times-table slip, dropped digit | Process fix: write bigger, show every line |
| `M` | Misread | Solved a different problem than the one asked | Read twice; restate the question first |
| `S` | Stopped short | Correct work, didn't finish or simplify | Build and run a finishing checklist |

**Classify into exactly one category.** The forced choice is what makes the log
useful — "a bit of both" tells you nothing and lets you avoid the uncomfortable
verdict, which is usually `A`.

Keep "what went wrong" to a single specific line. **"Forgot to flip the inequality
when dividing by a negative"** is useful. "Careless" is not — it names a feeling
rather than a mechanism, and you cannot drill a feeling.

---

## Weekly review — every Sunday, 20 minutes

1. **Count the codes.** Write the distribution: e.g. `C:2 A:9 R:4 M:1 S:0`.
2. **Find repeats.** Any specific skill appearing 3+ times becomes a named drill
   for the coming week — put it in the warm-up block of every session.
3. **Read the distribution.** It tells you what to change:

| Dominant code | Diagnosis | Change |
|---|---|---|
| `C` | Moving too fast | Add worked examples; slow the new-material block |
| `A` | **Fluency debt — the Calculus II failure mode** | Halt new material. Drill until automatic. |
| `R` | Process, not knowledge | Slow the hand. No mental steps. Bigger paper. |
| `M` | Attention/intake | Restate every problem in your own words before starting |
| `S` | No finishing routine | Write a checklist; run it on every problem |

4. **Mark fixes.** A skill that stops appearing for two consecutive weeks is
   genuinely repaired. Move it to SRS maintenance and stop drilling it.

---

## Why `A` is the one to watch

For a returning student with a strong pre-calculus record, `A` will dominate
early, and it is the whole reason this program exists.

An `A` error means you understood the problem completely and the execution broke
underneath you. That is exactly the failure that ends Calculus II: not confusion
about integration by parts, but a sign lost three lines into the algebra that
follows it. Concept errors are honest and self-announcing — you know when you
don't understand something. Algebra errors hide, because you *did* understand, and
so the natural conclusion is "careless mistake, I know this really." You do know
it. It just isn't free yet, and that is a different problem with a different fix.

The fix is never "be more careful." It is repetition until the step costs nothing.

**Rule:** if `A` exceeds 50% of your errors in any week, you do not start new
material the following week. That week is a drill week. This will feel like losing
ground and it is the opposite — it is the single decision that separates this
attempt from the last one.

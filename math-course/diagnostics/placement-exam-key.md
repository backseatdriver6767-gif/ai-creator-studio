## Section 1 — Arithmetic & Fraction Fluency

| # | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1 | `2` |
| 2 | `1/12` |
| 3 | `2/3` |
| 4 | `0` |
| 5 | `-7` |

**Notes.** Q4 is the classic sign trap: `-2^4` means `-(2^4) = -16`, while
`(-2)^4 = 16`. If you got `32` or `-32`, that is an exponent-precedence gap and it
will produce wrong derivatives later. Q5 requires working the innermost
parentheses first: `4 - 7 = -3`, then `3 - (-3) = 6`.

**Notes.** Q6: distribute the outer exponent first (`x^6 y^-4`), then subtract

## Section 3 — Linear Equations, Inequalities & Systems

| # | Answer |
|---|---|
| 11 | `x = 20` |
| 12 | `y = (2x - 12)/3` |
| 13 | `[-2, 5)` |
| 14 | `x = -1, y = 3` |
| 15 | `x = 4 or x = -3` |

**Notes.** Q13 is the one that separates people: dividing (or multiplying) an
inequality by a negative **reverses** the inequality sign. `-5 < -x ≤ 2` becomes
`5 > x ≥ -2`, i.e. `[-2, 5)`. Note the endpoint types swap along with the
direction.

## Section 4 — Factoring & Quadratics

| # | Answer |
|---|---|
| 16 | `3x(x - 2)(x + 2)` |
| 17 | `(3x + 4)(2x - 3)` |
| 18 | `x = 3 ± √5` |
| 19 | `(x - 2)(x² + 2x + 4)` |
| 20 | `y = (x + 5)² - 18` |

**Notes.** Q16 requires *completely* — pulling the GCF and stopping at
`3x(x² - 4)` is a partial credit answer at best; the difference of squares must be
taken. Q18 must be exact: `(6 ± √20)/2` simplifies to `3 ± √5`. Q20: half of 10 is
5, `5² = 25`, so `(x + 5)² - 25 + 7 = (x + 5)² - 18`.

**This section is the single best predictor in the whole diagnostic.** Completing
the square (Q20) *is* trigonometric substitution in Calculus II, and factoring is
every cancellation in every limit.

## Section 5 — Rational Expressions

| # | Answer |
|---|---|
| 21 | `(x + 3)/(x + 2)`, with `x ≠ 3` |
| 22 | `(2x + 1) / (x(x + 1))` |
| 23 | `-1/(3x)` |
| 24 | `x = 7` |
| 25 | `x² - x + 2 + 1/(x - 1)` |

**Notes.** Q23 is the most diagnostic problem on the exam. The numerator
`1/x - 1/3` combines to `(3 - x)/(3x)`; dividing by `(x - 3)` gives
`(3 - x) / (3x(x - 3))`, and recognizing `(3 - x) = -(x - 3)` collapses it to
`-1/(3x)`. That "factor out the negative to cancel" move appears in nearly every
difference-quotient problem in Calculus I. If you missed only this one in the
section, it still counts — flag it.

Q25 is synthetic or long division; the remainder of 1 must be reported.

## Section 6 — Functions

| # | Answer |
|---|---|
| 26 | `a² - a - 2` |
| 27 | `[2, 5) ∪ (5, ∞)` |
| 28 | `-2x² - 4x` |
| 29 | `f⁻¹(x) = (3x + 1)/(x - 2)` |
| 30 | Right 3 → vertical stretch by 2 → reflect across the x-axis → up 1 |

**Notes.** Q27 needs *both* conditions: the radical forces `x ≥ 2` and the
denominator forbids `x = 5`. Getting one but not the other is a miss. Q29:
cross-multiply, collect all x-terms on one side, factor out x. Q30: order matters —
horizontal shift acts on the input before the vertical operations act on the output.

## Section 7 — Exponentials & Logarithms

| # | Answer |
|---|---|
| 31 | `x = 4` |
| 32 | `-3` |
| 33 | `ln(x²√z / y)` |
| 34 | `x = 5` |
| 35 | `t = 20ln(5)` |

**Notes.** Q34 has an extraneous solution: `x² - 3x - 10 = 0` factors to
`(x - 5)(x + 2)`, but `x = -2` makes `log(x)` undefined and must be rejected. **If
you kept `x = -2`, count this wrong** — domain-checking after solving is a habit,
and its absence causes real errors later. Q35 must be exact; `t ≈ 32.19` alone is
not the answer asked for.

**Notes.** Q37 and Q38 are pure unit-circle recall — no derivation should have

second-quadrant solution is the most common trig-equation error there is.

## Placement rule

> **Start at the earliest unit fed by any section scoring 3 or below.**

Earliest, not average. A 38/40 with a 2/5 in Section 4 still starts at Week 5 —
that Section 4 gap is precisely the thing that ends Calculus II, and skipping it
because the overall score looks good is the exact mistake this program is built to
prevent.

Everything before your start point gets folded into the daily SRS deck rather than
studied as units. Nothing is skipped; the intact material is just maintained
instead of rebuilt.

---

## Interpreting the overall score

These are calibrations, not grades. Every one of them ends in the same place.

- **34–40.** The foundation is largely intact and this is a fast refresher.
  Expect the compressed track. Your risk is *overconfidence in Section 8* —
  trigonometry is the material that decays most, and a strong algebra score
  disguises it.
- **26–33.** Typical for a strong student fifteen years out, and the profile this
  program was designed around. The knowledge is there but not automatic. Phases
  1–3 will feel like remembering rather than learning, and they will go faster
  than the calendar suggests.
- **18–25.** Real gaps, and Phase 1 is genuinely necessary rather than a warm-up.
  This is a completely normal starting point and it is not a problem — it just
  means the early weeks are load-bearing. Do not compress them.
- **Below 18.** Start at Week 1 and treat the first six weeks as the actual
  course rather than a preliminary. Nothing about this score predicts your
  ceiling; it reflects fifteen years of disuse, which is exactly what it should
  reflect.

**The pattern that matters more than the total:** if Sections 1–5 are strong and
Section 8 is weak, you have found the reason Calculus II went badly. That is the
most common profile for someone with your history, and Phase 3's eight weeks exist
specifically for it.

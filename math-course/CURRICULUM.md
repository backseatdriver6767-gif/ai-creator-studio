# Curriculum Map

Six phases, 56 weeks, arithmetic through Calculus II. Every phase ends in a gate
exam at 85%. See `METHOD.md` for why the gate is where it is.

**Baseline track:** 6–8 hrs/week → 56 weeks (~13 months)
**Compressed track:** 10–12 hrs/week → 40 weeks (~9 months). Compression comes
from doubling session frequency, *not* from cutting practice or lowering gates.
Notes on what compresses safely are at the end.

---

## Phase 1 — The Algebra Engine (Weeks 1–6)

The reflex layer. Everything here must become automatic, not merely understood.
This phase is short in content and long in repetition, and that is intentional:
it is the phase that decides whether Calculus II works out.

| Week | Unit | Topics |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 01 | Signed numbers, order of operations, fraction arithmetic, complex fractions |
| 2 | 02 | Exponent laws, negative and rational exponents, scientific notation |
| 3 | 03 | Radicals, simplifying, rationalizing, radical equations |
| 4 | 04 | Linear equations, literal equations, inequalities, compound inequalities, absolute value |
| 5 | 05 | Factoring: GCF, difference of squares, trinomials, grouping, sum/difference of cubes |
| 6 | 06 | Quadratics: factoring, completing the square, quadratic formula, discriminant |

**Gate Exam A.** 90 minutes, no calculator, 85%.

**Why these six and nothing else:** every one is consumed directly by calculus.
Fraction arithmetic is the derivative quotient rule and every partial-fraction
decomposition. Exponent laws are the power rule. Factoring is every limit that
requires cancellation and every integrand you'll simplify. Completing the square
is trig substitution. None of this is review-for-the-sake-of-review.

---

## Phase 2 — Functions & Applied Geometry (Weeks 7–12)

Functions are the object calculus operates on. Geometry is compressed to only
what calculus consumes.

| Week | Unit | Topics |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | 07 | Function notation, evaluation, domain and range, piecewise functions |
| 8 | 08 | Graph transformations, symmetry, even/odd, composition, inverse functions |
| 9 | 09 | Polynomial behavior: end behavior, zeros, multiplicity, sketching |
| 10 | 10 | Rational functions: asymptotes, holes, sketching, rational inequalities |
| 11 | 11 | Exponential and logarithmic functions, laws of logs, solving, growth/decay models |
| 12 | 12 | Applied geometry: similar triangles, Pythagoras, area/volume/surface area, coordinate geometry, distance and midpoint |

**Gate Exam B.** 90 minutes, calculator permitted on Unit 11 problems only, 85%.

**Note on Unit 12:** this is the entire geometry curriculum, compressed to one
week. Formal proof geometry is cut (see README). What remains is the geometry that
shows up inside related-rates and optimization problems, which is where geometry
actually appears in calculus.

---

## Phase 3 — Trigonometry to Automaticity (Weeks 13–20)

**The most important phase in the program.** Eight weeks — longer than any other
pre-calculus phase — because trigonometry is the single largest cause of failure
in Calculus II, and because it is the material most thoroughly lost after fifteen
years.

| Week | Unit | Topics |
|---|---|---|
| 13 | 13 | Angles, radians, arc length, sector area, right-triangle trig |
| 14 | 14 | The unit circle — built, derived, then memorized cold |
| 15 | 15 | Graphs of sine and cosine: amplitude, period, phase shift, vertical shift |
| 16 | 16 | Graphs of tangent, secant, cosecant, cotangent |
| 17 | 17 | Fundamental identities: Pythagorean, reciprocal, quotient, even/odd |
| 18 | 18 | Sum/difference, double-angle, half-angle identities; verifying identities |
| 19 | 19 | Inverse trig functions, domains and ranges, trigonometric equations |
| 20 | 20 | Law of Sines, Law of Cosines, applications, vectors in the plane |

**Gate Exam C.** 120 minutes, no calculator, 85%. Includes a separate **unit
circle speed test: all 16 standard angles, sine/cosine/tangent, under 3 minutes,
100% required.** This sub-gate is pass/fail and non-negotiable.

**Why the speed test is absolute:** in Calculus II you will evaluate trig
functions at standard angles constantly, inside problems whose actual difficulty
lies elsewhere. Every second spent reconstructing `cos(5π/6)` is a second not
spent on the integral, and every reconstruction is a chance to introduce a sign
error. This must be free.

## Phase 4 — Precalculus Glue (Weeks 21–28)

The connective tissue, plus the first real approach to limits.

| Week | Unit | Topics |
|---|---|---|
| 21 | 21 | Systems of equations, nonlinear systems, partial fraction decomposition |
| 22 | 22 | Sequences, series, summation notation, arithmetic and geometric series |
| 23 | 23 | Polar coordinates, polar graphs, complex numbers, De Moivre's theorem |
| 24 | 24 | Parametric equations, conic sections (recognition and standard forms) |
| 25 | 25 | Limits: intuition, tables, graphs, one-sided limits |
| 26 | 26 | Limit laws, algebraic evaluation, indeterminate forms, cancellation and conjugates |
| 27 | 27 | Continuity, Intermediate Value Theorem, limits at infinity and asymptotic behavior |
| 28 | 28 | Consolidation, mixed review, gate preparation |

**Gate Exam D.** 120 minutes, 85%. This is the true "calculus-ready" checkpoint.

**Note on Unit 21:** partial fraction decomposition is taught here rather than in
Calculus II, where it is normally introduced. Learning it as pure algebra now —
with no integral attached and no time pressure — removes one of Calculus II's
sharpest spikes before you ever get there.

## Phase 5 — Calculus I (Weeks 29–42)

| Week | Unit | Topics |
|---|---|---|
| 29 | 29 | The derivative: definition, limit definition, interpretation as rate and slope |
| 30 | 30 | Power, constant, sum rules; differentiability vs. continuity |
| 31 | 31 | Product and quotient rules |
| 32 | 32 | The chain rule (allow two weeks of practice — this is the workhorse) |
| 33 | 33 | Derivatives of trig functions |
| 34 | 34 | Derivatives of exponential and logarithmic functions, logarithmic differentiation |
| 35 | 35 | Implicit differentiation, derivatives of inverse trig functions |
| 36 | 36 | Related rates |
| 37 | 37 | Linear approximation, differentials, L'Hôpital's rule |
| 38 | 38 | Extrema, critical points, Mean Value Theorem |
| 39 | 39 | First and second derivative tests, concavity, curve sketching |
| 40 | 40 | Optimization |
| 41 | 41 | Antiderivatives, indefinite integrals, u-substitution |
| 42 | 42 | Riemann sums, definite integrals, Fundamental Theorem of Calculus |

**Gate Exam E.** 150 minutes, two parts (no-calculator and calculator), 85%.

## Phase 6 — Calculus II (Weeks 43–56)

The course you withdrew from. By this point every technique below rests on
material you have already driven to 85% or better.

| Week | Unit | Topics |
|---|---|---|
| 43 | 43 | Integration by parts |
| 44 | 44 | Trigonometric integrals |
| 45 | 45 | Trigonometric substitution |
| 46 | 46 | Partial fractions in integration (the algebra was done in Week 21) |
| 47 | 47 | Improper integrals, numerical integration |
| 48 | 48 | Area between curves, volumes by disks and washers |
| 49 | 49 | Volumes by cylindrical shells, arc length, surface area |
| 50 | 50 | Work, center of mass, applications of integration |
| 51 | 51 | Sequences, series, convergence and divergence |
| 52 | 52 | Integral, comparison, and limit comparison tests |
| 53 | 53 | Alternating series, absolute convergence, ratio and root tests |
| 54 | 54 | Power series, radius and interval of convergence |
| 55 | 55 | Taylor and Maclaurin series, applications |
| 56 | 56 | Parametric and polar calculus, consolidation |

**Final Exam.** 180 minutes, comprehensive, two parts, 85%.

## Compression notes

To run the 40-week track, compress as follows:

**Safe to compress** (roughly halve the calendar time by doubling weekly hours):
- Phase 1, Weeks 1–3 — if the diagnostic scores 4+/5 on Sections 1 and 2
- Phase 2, Weeks 9–10 — polynomial and rational behavior is largely graph-reading
- Phase 4, Weeks 23–24 — polar, parametric, and conics are recognition-level here
- Phase 5, Weeks 29–31 — the early derivative rules are mechanically simple

**Never compress:**
- Weeks 5–6 (factoring, quadratics) — the entire program's load-bearing wall
- Weeks 13–20 (all of trigonometry) — the actual reason Calculus II failed
- Week 32 (chain rule) — the most-used and most-miscarried rule in calculus
- Weeks 51–55 (series) — the hardest material in the sequence, full stop

**Never compressible under any circumstance:** the daily 10-minute SRS block, the
weekly mixed-review session, and the 85% gate threshold. Compression means more
hours per week, never fewer repetitions and never a lower bar. A phase entered on
a compromised gate costs more time downstream than it saved.
